**about_the_game 正文:**

> WARDOGS is an All-Out Warfare FPS that combines tactical gunplay and combined arms combat with building and destruction mechanics in a large-scale, up to 100-player militaristic sandbox.
>
> The game mode - Inspired by ‘King of the Hill’ - has three teams fight for control of randomized 2x2km ‘Control Zones’ within larger maps. The team with the most players within the Control Zone earns points - the first team to reach 100 points wins the match. The variables in zone location and individual player tactics mean no match ever plays out the same.
>
> In WARDOGS – Every. Decision. Matters.
>
> Every player starts their journey with $10,000. Each life, you purchase a custom loadout from a wide selection of weapons, gear, utility, and vehicles to help your team win the match.
>
> Every teamplay action you perform rewards cash. From reviving squadmates, transporting friendlies to the zone, or controlling the objective. We don’t just encourage teamplay; we reward it. It’s the player’s choice how they want to play, earn and spend their profits.
>
> Cash persists from match to match, so spending big on better gear, equipment, or a vehicle at the right time has the potential to turn the tide of battle and could yield massive returns.
>
> You Choose How You Want to Play & Profit.
>
> There’s no one way to play, earn and progress. Instead, WARDOGS gives players the freedom to take on the Control Zone in any number of ways. Whether you’re a lone wolf helping out your team or part of a dedicated squad, how you approach the battlefield is entirely up to you.
>
> You could chase the 'Hot Zone' to earn double the cash, purchase a ghillie suit and snipe from a distance, or ferry supplies to teammates by helicopter. Additionally, you could choose to fortify a strategic chokepoint by building your own forward operating base, or if you're feeling destructive... man an artillery tank and rain down destruction on the enemy.
>
> There are no set rules of engagement. It’s ultimately your choice how you want to approach the battlefield.
>
> Bring the Chaos to Tactical Warfare.
> Destroy apartment blocks with rocket launchers or tear your way through towns in heavily armoured tanks. The world around you reacts to your presence. Conversely, strategize with your teammates to build upon and fortify key locations within the map for a tactical advantage.
>
> Every Moment in WARDOGS is 100% Player Driven and tied together with local voice chat to either talk tactics with teammates or troll enemy players. This is not another ‘Battle Royale’. This is not another ‘Extraction Shooter’. WARDOGS is a new Tactical take on the All-Out Warfare FPS genre that rewards decision making, communication and teamwork – and allows you to play how you want within a modern militaristic setting.

> BULKHEAD describes Cash as a renewable seasonal resource and Gold Bars as long-term value. > Gold Bars and cosmetics remain permanently on the account across seasons. Player Skills are also planned as permanent progression. > The WARDOGS wipe guide documents the reset boundary. Exact season duration, conversion rates, caps, and the first reset date remain open even though the overall cycle is confirmed.

> Black Market, businesses, and the Vault
>
> The Black Market is planned as an optional way to use earnings outside a match. BULKHEAD discusses bribing an arms dealer to deliver weapons or ammunition, placing a wager on a difficult combat goal, and investing in businesses that produce items while the player is offline. [03]
>
> The presentation also describes buying out a weapon or vehicle manufacturer to create preferred loadouts more cheaply than an in-match vendor. A player with high-level access could sell supply crates to other players with a markup. These are design plans, not verified live prices or a promise that unrestricted trading will exist on launch day.
>
> Items, weapons, or vehicles obtained through the Black Market can enter a personal Vault and be redeemed free in future matches. Vault capacity is planned to be capped but permanently upgradeable. The Black Market and Vault guide keeps the confirmed design separate from unknown inventory, pricing, and season rules.
>
> More maps, weapons, vehicles, and objectives
>
> Steam says the full version should be broader and more polished, with more maps, weapons, vehicles, and gameplay variety. Expanded weapon categories, additional objective variations, and fighter jets are listed as examples of broader additions. [01]
>
> None of those statements supplies an item count. They also do not establish an order. A WARDOGS roadmap graphic that assigns a jet to "Season 2" or names three launch maps would be invented unless BULKHEAD publishes that schedule.
>
> Future entity pages need stable evidence. A weapon page requires an official name and public statistics. A vehicle page needs a name, seats, price, and repeatable build data. A map page needs an official title and location labels. Until those fields exist, category guides remain more accurate than placeholder databases.
>
> Community feedback and seasonal updates
>
> Steam says the studio will gather feedback through playtests, social channels, and Steam, while monitoring gameplay data for balance and economy changes. Seasonal updates are planned to respond to player behavior. This makes the roadmap adjustable by design. [01]
>
> Feedback does not mean every popular request becomes a commitment. BULKHEAD's separate discussion of killcam shows the studio may keep a main-mode design choice while offering a different community-server preset. A roadmap update should therefore record what changed, why it changed, and which mode or server type receives the result.
>
> The current version 1.0 target is roughly 24 months after Early Access begins. It is planning language, not a fixed release date; development scope, stability, testing, and player feedback can move it. [05]
