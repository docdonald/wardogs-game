The team with the most players within the Control Zone earns points - the first team to reach 100 points wins the match. The variables in zone location and individual player tactics mean no match ever plays out the same.

> In WARDOGS – Every. Decision. Matters.
>
> Every player starts their journey with $10,000. Each life, you purchase a custom loadout from a wide selection of weapons, gear, utility, and vehicles to help your team win the match.
>
> Every teamplay action you perform rewards cash. From reviving squadmates, transporting friendlies to the zone, or controlling the objective. We don’t just encourage teamplay; we reward it. It’s the player’s choice how they want to play, earn and spend their profits.

> Cash persists from match to match, so spending big on better gear, equipment, or a vehicle at the right time has the potential to turn the tide of battle and could yield massive returns.
>
> You Choose How You Want to Play & Profit.
>
> There’s no one way to play, earn and progress. Instead, WARDOGS gives players the freedom to take on the Control Zone in any number of ways. Whether you’re a lone wolf helping out your team or part of a dedicated squad, how you approach the battlefield is entirely up to you.
>
> You could chase the 'Hot Zone' to earn double the cash, purchase a ghillie suit and snipe from a distance, or ferry supplies to teammates by helicopter. Additionally, you could choose to fortify a strategic chokepoint by building your own forward operating base, or if you're feeling destructive... man an artillery tank and rain down destruction on the enemy.
>
> There are no set rules of engagement. It’s ultimately your choice how you want to approach the battlefield.
>
> Bring the Chaos to Tactical Warfare.
> Destroy apartment blocks with rocket launchers or tear your way through towns in heavily armoured tanks. The world around you reacts to your presence. Conversely, strategize with your teammates to build upon and fortify key locations within the map for a tactical advantage.
>
> Every Moment in WARDOGS is 100% Player Driven and tied together with local voice chat to either talk tactics with teammates or troll enemy players. This is not another ‘Battle Royale’. This is not another ‘Extraction Shooter’. WARDOGS is a new Tactical take on the All-Out Warfare FPS genre that rewards decision making, communication and teamwork – and allows you to play how you want within a modern militaristic setting.

> Local voice and squad communication are different layers
>
> WARDOGS proximity chat is tied to nearby players. Squad communication is described as an additional benefit of squadding up. The public material does not explain the exact interface, but the wording supports a useful conceptual split. [01]
>
> Local voice handles the immediate environment: warning a nearby player about a vehicle, asking for a revive, coordinating entry through a doorway, or speaking to someone who is not in the same squad. A squad channel can preserve coordination when members spread across a wider route or ride in separate vehicles.
>
> That difference matters in a 100-player, three-team match. One global channel would be difficult to read, while squad-only voice would prevent spontaneous coordination with nearby teammates. WARDOGS proximity chat gives the local battlefield a social layer without making it the only announced communication tool.
>
> The exact channel precedence is unknown. We cannot yet confirm whether both can be heard simultaneously, whether players switch manually, or whether vehicle interiors change voice behavior.
>
> A Closed Alpha field report describes passengers speaking with a helicopter pilot during transport, which supports the announced local-social use in one real test. It does not establish the final range, channel controls, or vehicle-audio rules. [04]

> Why proximity matters in Control Zone play
>
> The Control Zone awards one point every 30 seconds to the team with the strongest presence. That creates time-sensitive situations among players who may never have joined the same squad. [01]
>
> WARDOGS proximity chat can support short, place-specific messages: “wait for the next count,” “third team south,” “medic upstairs,” or “seats available.” Those calls are useful because the listener is already close enough to act. The system may also help players coordinate building, transport, and defensive positions without requiring a formal group before every interaction.
>
> This is a practical inference from the confirmed game rules and local-voice feature. It is not evidence of a particular communication meta, audio range, or player behavior. Live matches will determine whether people use the channel for concise information, social play, faction banter, or noise.

> Enemy interaction changes the social boundary
>
> Team17 says local voice can be used to talk tactics with teammates or troll enemy players. The Top Questions announcement similarly presents proximity chat as part of the player-driven sandbox. That means WARDOGS proximity chat is not described as friendly-only. [02] [03]
>
> Cross-team audibility can create bluffing, warnings, bargaining, taunts, accidental information leaks, and memorable encounters. It can also create harassment or unwanted noise. The studio says community-server administrators can moderate their own spaces and developers can act where needed, but a complete reporting and enforcement workflow has not been published. [05]
>
> Players should therefore avoid assuming that “enemy interaction confirmed” answers every rule. It does not tell us whether all opponents can always hear each other, whether downed or dead players remain audible, whether distance falloff is directional, or whether a server can change the setting.

> The developers have said players can construct loudspeakers to broadcast faction propaganda at enemies. That fits the broader building and player-driven sandbox, but it should not be confused with ordinary WARDOGS proximity chat. [01]

> Practical communication without invented controls
>
> Even before keybinds are published, the confirmed structure supports several communication principles: [01]
>
> Use local voice for local facts. Direction, danger, seats, medical help, and the next objective check are immediately actionable.

> Keep persistent plans in squad communication. A route or multi-stage vehicle task may outlast local range.
>
> Assume nearby enemies may hear you. Official material explicitly allows enemy interaction, so sensitive plans may belong elsewhere.
>
> Name the subject before the detail. “Tank, east road” is easier to parse than a long explanation in a crowded fight.
