**about_the_game 正文:**

> WARDOGS is an All-Out Warfare FPS that combines tactical gunplay and combined arms combat with building and destruction mechanics in a large-scale, up to 100-player militaristic sandbox.
>
> The game mode - Inspired by ‘King of the Hill’ - has three teams fight for control of randomized 2x2km ‘Control Zones’ within larger maps. The team with the most players within the Control Zone earns points - the first team to reach 100 points wins the match. The variables in zone location and individual player tactics mean no match ever plays out the same.
>
> In WARDOGS – Every. Decision. Matters.
>
> Every player starts their journey with $10,000. Each life, you purchase a custom loadout from a wide selection of weapons, gear, utility, and vehicles to help your team win the match.
>
> Every teamplay action you perform rewards cash. From reviving squadmates, transporting friendlies to the zone, or controlling the objective. We don’t just encourage teamplay; we reward it. It’s the player’s choice how they want to play, earn and spend their profits.
>
> Cash persists from match to match, so spending big on better gear, equipment, or a vehicle at the right time has the potential to turn the tide of battle and could yield massive returns.
>
> You Choose How You Want to Play & Profit.
>
> There’s no one way to play, earn and progress. Instead, WARDOGS gives players the freedom to take on the Control Zone in any number of ways. Whether you’re a lone wolf helping out your team or part of a dedicated squad, how you approach the battlefield is entirely up to you.
>
> You could chase the 'Hot Zone' to earn double the cash, purchase a ghillie suit and snipe from a distance, or ferry supplies to teammates by helicopter. Additionally, you could choose to fortify a strategic chokepoint by building your own forward operating base, or if you're feeling destructive... man an artillery tank and rain down destruction on the enemy.
>
> There are no set rules of engagement. It’s ultimately your choice how you want to approach the battlefield.
>
> Bring the Chaos to Tactical Warfare.
> Destroy apartment blocks with rocket launchers or tear your way through towns in heavily armoured tanks. The world around you reacts to your presence. Conversely, strategize with your teammates to build upon and fortify key locations within the map for a tactical advantage.
>
> Every Moment in WARDOGS is 100% Player Driven and tied together with local voice chat to either talk tactics with teammates or troll enemy players. This is not another ‘Battle Royale’. This is not another ‘Extraction Shooter’. WARDOGS is a new Tactical take on the All-Out Warfare FPS genre that rewards decision making, communication and teamwork – and allows you to play how you want within a modern militaristic setting.

> What “unlimited squad size” confirms
>
> The developer’s wording is designed around playing with friends without a small arbitrary cap. It means players should not assume the common pattern where a larger group must split into several disconnected four-person units. [01]
>
> It does not establish an exact technical maximum. Servers have finite player counts, factions may not always have equal populations, and the official FAQ describes seamless drop-in and drop-out. A real squad may therefore change size during a session even if the system has no published hard limit.
>
> The safest reference wording is: WARDOGS squads are advertised as unlimited in size, within the practical limits of the match and whatever group interface ships. A later build-specific guide should record party creation, invitations, faction selection, late joining, reconnect behavior, and whether a group remains together between servers.
>

> Squad communication and local voice serve different jobs
>
> WARDOGS squads receive communication tools, while the game also supports proximity chat. The public sources do not show the final channel selector, yet the two announcements imply different ranges of coordination. [01]
>
> Local voice can connect nearby players who may not share a squad. Squad communication can keep a continuing group coordinated after its members spread out. A pilot, forward infantry element, and support vehicle may need the same plan even when they are no longer close enough for local speech.
>
> The official material has not confirmed command channels, squad-leader-only radio, text chat, pings, map drawing, voice range, or simultaneous channel behavior. These are important questions, not safe assumptions borrowed from Squad, Battlefield, Arma, or another large-scale shooter.
>
> How WARDOGS vehicle locking fits the system
>
> Vehicle locking is one of the three named benefits of WARDOGS squads. The likely purpose is coordination: a group that buys or organizes around a vehicle can control access rather than losing seats or the driver position during a planned task. [01]
>
> Only the existence of the feature is confirmed. Public official sources do not yet answer:
>
> who owns the lock—the purchaser, driver, leader, or squad;
>
> whether every seat or only control seats can be restricted;
>
> whether the whole faction can be allowed temporarily;
>
> what happens when the owner disconnects or changes squad;
>
> whether a damaged or abandoned vehicle unlocks automatically;
>
> whether players can remove unauthorized occupants;
>
> whether servers can change the rule.
>
> Those details decide whether vehicle locking protects coordination or strands useful assets. They should be tested before this page becomes a button-by-button guide.
>
> Squads do not create fixed classes
>
> The developer FAQ says WARDOGS has no set classes. Players buy equipment, medical supplies, building items, larger backpacks, weapons, and vehicles to create the role they want for a life. WARDOGS squads therefore organize people, but do not publicly impose a fixed composition. [03]
>
> A group can still divide responsibilities: transport, medical support, construction, reconnaissance, anti-vehicle work, or infantry presence. Those jobs are chosen through equipment and behavior rather than a mandatory squad template. The Flexible Roles guide explains this distinction, while XP Progression Tracks covers role-based growth.
>
> Because teamplay actions earn cash, a support role can be economically productive. The official FAQ even says roles that contribute to teamplay will be the most profitable. A WARDOGS squad can use that incentive to coordinate complementary jobs without declaring one permanent class lineup.
