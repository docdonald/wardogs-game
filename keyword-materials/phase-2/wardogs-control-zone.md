> WARDOGS is an All-Out Warfare FPS that combines tactical gunplay and combined arms combat with building and destruction mechanics in a large-scale, up to 100-player militaristic sandbox.
>
> The game mode - Inspired by ‘King of the Hill’ - has three teams fight for control of randomized 2x2km ‘Control Zones’ within larger maps. The team with the most players within the Control Zone earns points - the first team to reach 100 points wins the match. The variables in zone location and individual player tactics mean no match ever plays out the same.
>
> In WARDOGS – Every. Decision. Matters.
>
> Every player starts their journey with $10,000. Each life, you purchase a custom loadout from a wide selection of weapons, gear, utility, and vehicles to help your team win the match.
>
> Every teamplay action you perform rewards cash. From reviving squadmates, transporting friendlies to the zone, or controlling the objective. We don’t just encourage teamplay; we reward it. It’s the player’s choice how they want to play, earn and spend their profits.
>
> Cash persists from match to match, so spending big on better gear, equipment, or a vehicle at the right time has the potential to turn the tide of battle and could yield massive returns.
>
> You Choose How You Want to Play & Profit.
>
> There’s no one way to play, earn and progress. Instead, WARDOGS gives players the freedom to take on the Control Zone in any number of ways. Whether you’re a lone wolf helping out your team or part of a dedicated squad, how you approach the battlefield is entirely up to you.
>
> You could chase the 'Hot Zone' to earn double the cash, purchase a ghillie suit and snipe from a distance, or ferry supplies to teammates by helicopter. Additionally, you could choose to fortify a strategic chokepoint by building your own forward operating base, or if you're feeling destructive... man an artillery tank and rain down destruction on the enemy.
>
> There are no set rules of engagement. It’s ultimately your choice how you want to approach the battlefield.
>
> Bring the Chaos to Tactical Warfare.
> Destroy apartment blocks with rocket launchers or tear your way through towns in heavily armoured tanks. The world around you reacts to your presence. Conversely, strategize with your teammates to build upon and fortify key locations within the map for a tactical advantage.
>
> Every Moment in WARDOGS is 100% Player Driven and tied together with local voice chat to either talk tactics with teammates or troll enemy players. This is not another ‘Battle Royale’. This is not another ‘Extraction Shooter’. WARDOGS is a new Tactical take on the All-Out Warfare FPS genre that rewards decision making, communication and teamwork – and allows you to play how you want within a modern militaristic setting.

> How WARDOGS Control Zone scoring works
>
> WARDOGS Control Zone scoring is based on player presence. At each 30-second check, the game compares how many members of each team are inside the objective. The largest group receives one point. A team reaches the win condition at 100. [01]
>
> The rule creates a useful separation between combat and scoring. A sniper outside the boundary may protect the push, and a helicopter pilot can move players into position, but neither replaces the bodies required inside the zone. A squad that wins a firefight and then leaves the objective can still surrender the next point to a rival force arriving from another direction.
>
> Official sources have not published the tie-break rule. If two teams have the same presence when the interval ends, the game might pause the award, use another condition, or apply a rule not yet documented. The wiki will not infer that behavior from a trailer frame.
>
> The arithmetic also needs a boundary. One point every 30 seconds means 100 uncontested scoring intervals equal 50 minutes after scoring begins. That is a calculation from the published rules, not a confirmed match duration. Setup time, tied checks, pauses, overtime, or future balance changes could make an actual round shorter or longer.
>
> Why three teams change the objective
>
> A two-team objective usually creates one main opposition: attackers read the defenders, while defenders read the attackers. The WARDOGS Control Zone has a third force that can pressure either side or ignore their fight and add more players to the scoring area. [01]
>
> Imagine Lonestar and Valkyra trading fire on the western edge. Manticore does not need to finish both groups to matter. It can enter from the south, move enough players across the boundary, and take the next scoring check while the other teams are still focused on each other. The victory rule rewards that choice directly.
>
> This is a tactical implication of the confirmed team and scoring structure, not a promise that every match will develop the same way. Spawn rules, player distribution, vehicle access, terrain, and communication will decide how often a third-team entry succeeds.
>
> A 2 × 2 km objective inside a 256 km² map
>
> The battlefield around the WARDOGS Control Zone is much larger than the active objective. Official material describes a randomized 2 × 2 km area inside a 256 km² map. That relationship concentrates the match without making the surrounding terrain irrelevant. [01]
>
> Roads, rivers, industrial sites, villages, and ridgelines can determine how a team reaches the objective. Helicopters and ground transport shorten the trip. Builders can fortify useful ground, while launchers and armored vehicles can remove cover.
>
> The random placement changes which part of the larger map matters in a given round. One location may reward fast air movement; another may make bridges, streets, or dense buildings the practical routes into the objective. The official rules support that broad conclusion, but they do not yet provide a list of every possible zone center or boundary.
>
> See Battlefield Scale for the confirmed dimensions and Kolchia and PV-1 for the setting without speculative location names.
>
> The Hot Zone is a multiplier, not a second match
>
> Inside the wider objective is a smaller Hot Zone that shifts during play. A player inside it counts double toward team presence and earns double cash. That can change the next scoring comparison without replacing the main WARDOGS Control Zone. [01]
>
> Suppose Valkyra has 12 players in the wider area and Lonestar has 10. If two of Lonestar's players are inside the Hot Zone, their weighted presence becomes 12 under the published double-count rule. What happens to an exact tie is still unknown, but the example shows why a small group can change the balance of the larger objective.
>
> The Hot Zone also concentrates economic risk. It promises double cash, yet it is likely to attract players from all three teams because its objective weight is equally valuable. Exact dimensions, movement timing, cash values, and user-interface cues have not been published. The dedicated Hot Zone guide tracks those details separately so this page can stay focused on the main mode.
>
> Current test builds also connect local Towers to the Hot Zone. Squads collect match-specific digits at active Towers and can use the completed sequence to influence where the high-value area moves. The WARDOGS Tower code guide documents that workflow without presenting an old match code as a permanent answer.
>
> Cash rewards connect support work to the score
>
> WARDOGS rewards more than eliminations. Official examples include reviving teammates, transporting friendlies, spotting, and controlling the objective. Cash persists between matches and pays for later weapons, gear, utility, and vehicles. [01]
>
> That economy gives work outside the boundary a route back into the WARDOGS Control Zone. A pilot who delivers a squad, a medic who returns a player to the fight, or a transport driver who keeps reinforcements moving can help change the next count. The published reward examples support those roles, although exact payouts and item prices remain unknown.
>
> Victory points and cash remain separate. One decides which team wins the match; the other remains on the player's account. Read How to Earn Cash for reward actions and Persistent Cash for the account-level rules.
>
> What one Closed Alpha report observed
>
> PC Gamer's August Closed Alpha report describes the published rules operating in an actual test: three teams contested the zone, players in the Hot Zone counted as two, and a helicopter pilot could finish near the top of the scoreboard through transport work despite recording no kills. The writer reported earning about $35,000 in that support session. Treat those results as one dated field report, not a payout table or launch-build benchmark. [05]
>
> Field guide based on the published rules
>
> The following WARDOGS Control Zone guidance is editorial reasoning from confirmed rules, not a verified Early Access meta. [01]
>
> Watch the interval, not only the firefight. Arriving before the count can matter more than chasing an opponent outside the boundary.
>
> Preserve useful presence. A player alive inside contributes to the comparison; a low-value duel can remove that weight before the next check.
>
> Keep a route open. A team cut off from reinforcements may win one engagement and lose the following count.
>
> Track the third team. Pressure on one edge does not explain the whole objective. A quiet route may hide the unobserved faction.
>
> Use the Hot Zone deliberately. Feeding expensive lives into the multiplier without support may improve neither score nor balance.
>
> These principles follow from the rules, but they still need testing against public builds. Respawn locations, map flow, vehicle prices, time-to-kill, building limits, and squad tools can change which choice is best.
