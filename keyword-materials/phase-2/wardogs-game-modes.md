> WARDOGS is an All-Out Warfare FPS that combines tactical gunplay and combined arms combat with building and destruction mechanics in a large-scale, up to 100-player militaristic sandbox.
>
> The game mode - Inspired by ‘King of the Hill’ - has three teams fight for control of randomized 2x2km ‘Control Zones’ within larger maps. The team with the most players within the Control Zone earns points - the first team to reach 100 points wins the match. The variables in zone location and individual player tactics mean no match ever plays out the same.
>
> In WARDOGS – Every. Decision. Matters.
>
> Every player starts their journey with $10,000. Each life, you purchase a custom loadout from a wide selection of weapons, gear, utility, and vehicles to help your team win the match.
>
> Every teamplay action you perform rewards cash. From reviving squadmates, transporting friendlies to the zone, or controlling the objective. We don’t just encourage teamplay; we reward it. It’s the player’s choice how they want to play, earn and spend their profits.
>
> Cash persists from match to match, so spending big on better gear, equipment, or a vehicle at the right time has the potential to turn the tide of battle and could yield massive returns.
>
> You Choose How You Want to Play & Profit.
>
> There’s no one way to play, earn and progress. Instead, WARDOGS gives players the freedom to take on the Control Zone in any number of ways. Whether you’re a lone wolf helping out your team or part of a dedicated squad, how you approach the battlefield is entirely up to you.
>
> You could chase the 'Hot Zone' to earn double the cash, purchase a ghillie suit and snipe from a distance, or ferry supplies to teammates by helicopter. Additionally, you could choose to fortify a strategic chokepoint by building your own forward operating base, or if you're feeling destructive... man an artillery tank and rain down destruction on the enemy.
>
> There are no set rules of engagement. It’s ultimately your choice how you want to approach the battlefield.
>
> Bring the Chaos to Tactical Warfare.
> Destroy apartment blocks with rocket launchers or tear your way through towns in heavily armoured tanks. The world around you reacts to your presence. Conversely, strategize with your teammates to build upon and fortify key locations within the map for a tactical advantage.
>
> Every Moment in WARDOGS is 100% Player Driven and tied together with local voice chat to either talk tactics with teammates or troll enemy players. This is not another ‘Battle Royale’. This is not another ‘Extraction Shooter’. WARDOGS is a new Tactical take on the All-Out Warfare FPS genre that rewards decision making, communication and teamwork – and allows you to play how you want within a modern militaristic setting.

> Why WARDOGS is not an extraction shooter
>
> An extraction match is normally organized around leaving the play space successfully. Valuable items matter because the player must carry them to an exit or satisfy another extraction rule. Survival protects the haul, and a failed exit can erase the raid's gain. [01]
>
> WARDOGS directs players toward the Control Zone. Every 30 seconds, the faction with the largest presence inside the zone earns one point. The first faction to 100 wins. Players can spend ten minutes on one life or remain for several matches, and the developer FAQ says they can drop in and out of the wider battle.
>
> Cash still raises the cost of dying. Each life uses purchased weapons, equipment, utility, or a vehicle. But there is no published requirement to loot a map and reach an extraction point to bank that Cash. Team actions such as reviving, transport, objective control, and logistics generate rewards inside an ongoing faction battle.
>
> The persistent Cash guide explains the account economy. Economic risk alone does not change the objective into extraction.
>
> Why WARDOGS is not a battle royale
>
> Battle royale games commonly narrow a large lobby toward one surviving player or squad. WARDOGS instead divides the lobby across Lonestar, Valkyra, and Manticore. A faction wins by accumulating objective points, not by eliminating every opponent permanently. [01]
>
> The published design expects players to die, purchase another loadout, and return. That continuing flow is essential to a potentially long three-way Control Zone fight. A player who dies has paid an economic cost, but the faction has not been removed from the match.
>
> The map structure also serves a different purpose. A randomized 2x2 km Control Zone appears within a much larger battlefield and focuses the current fight. It is not described as a lethal circle whose primary job is to eliminate players outside a shrinking safe area.
>
> Read the Control Zone rules for the scoring intervals, point threshold, and three-team population check.
>
> Where the extraction comparison still helps
>
> WARDOGS borrows one source of tension that extraction players will recognize: equipment has account-level opportunity cost. Spending heavily on a rifle, armor, utility, or vehicle makes the current life more capable while exposing more Cash to an early loss. [01]
>
> That pressure can affect route choice and timing. A player may avoid an unnecessary fight, wait for transport, or choose a cheaper role after repeated losses. The similarity ends at the decision pressure. WARDOGS does not adopt an extraction win condition, a mandatory personal exit, or a published loot-stash raid structure.
>
> Seasonal resets add another familiar-looking feature, but their function is explicit. Cash and XP reset at season end, remaining Cash automatically converts into Gold Bars, and long-term rewards remain. The WARDOGS wipe guide covers that cycle without calling it a Tarkov-style wipe.
>
> What Tactical All-Out Warfare means here
>
> The official wording combines large-scale infantry and vehicles with deliberate loadout choices, construction, destruction, proximity voice, and a persistent economy. "Tactical" describes the decisions and consequences. "All-Out Warfare" describes the scale and combined-arms sandbox. [01]
>
> It is also inspired by Arma 3 King of the Hill. That influence is visible in the contested zone and three-team structure, but WARDOGS packages the rules as a standalone game with its own economy and progression. The King of the Hill origins guide documents the relationship without treating the games as identical.
>
> BULKHEAD says the gunplay should sit between slower tactical decisions and reactive, responsive shooting. That position explains why the studio rejects a simple milsim-versus-arcade label and why the genre answer should be based on rules rather than visual tone.
>
> What happens after a death
>
> The public description says players purchase a custom loadout for each life. Dying removes the value committed to that life, while the remaining account Cash persists. The player can return with another purchase and continue helping the same faction. [01]
>
> Exact respawn timers, spawn locations, medical states, and recovery rules can vary by build and are not fully documented. The developer has said there is no squad-spawn system, so returning to a squad requires travel rather than appearing directly on a teammate.
>
> That loop reinforces the classification. Death hurts, but it does not end the whole match for the player. The team objective continues, and another life can still earn Cash or change the Control Zone count.
