>
> Every player starts their journey with $10,000. Each life, you purchase a custom loadout from a wide selection of weapons, gear, utility, and vehicles to help your team win the match.

> Cash persists from match to match, so spending big on better gear, equipment, or a vehicle at the right time has the potential to turn the tide of battle and could yield massive returns.
>
> You Choose How You Want to Play & Profit.
>
> There’s no one way to play, earn and progress. Instead, WARDOGS gives players the freedom to take on the Control Zone in any number of ways. Whether you’re a lone wolf helping out your team or part of a dedicated squad, how you approach the battlefield is entirely up to you.
>
> You could chase the 'Hot Zone' to earn double the cash, purchase a ghillie suit and snipe from a distance, or ferry supplies to teammates by helicopter. Additionally, you could choose to fortify a strategic chokepoint by building your own forward operating base, or if you're feeling destructive... man an artillery tank and rain down destruction on the enemy.
>
> There are no set rules of engagement. It’s ultimately your choice how you want to approach the battlefield.
>
> Bring the Chaos to Tactical Warfare.
> Destroy apartment blocks with rocket launchers or tear your way through towns in heavily armoured tanks. The world around you reacts to your presence. Conversely, strategize with your teammates to build upon and fortify key locations within the map for a tactical advantage.
>
> Every Moment in WARDOGS is 100% Player Driven and tied together with local voice chat to either talk tactics with teammates or troll enemy players. This is not another ‘Battle Royale’. This is not another ‘Extraction Shooter’. WARDOGS is a new Tactical take on the All-Out Warfare FPS genre that rewards decision making, communication and teamwork – and allows you to play how you want within a modern militaristic setting.

> How double objective weight changes a score check
>
> The main Control Zone compares the presence of Valkyra, Lonestar, and Manticore. Every 30 seconds, the team with the most players in that objective receives one point. A WARDOGS Hot Zone player contributes twice the normal presence while inside the smaller area. [01]
>
> Consider a simple example. Valkyra has 13 players elsewhere in the Control Zone. Lonestar has nine players there and three more in the WARDOGS Hot Zone. Those three count as six, giving Lonestar a weighted total of 15. Under the published rules, Lonestar would have the strongest presence at that moment even though Valkyra has more physical players inside the wider objective.

> The example explains arithmetic, not every edge case. Official sources have not explained exact tie handling, whether a player crossing the boundary at the instant of a check is included, or how disconnected players are treated. Those questions need a public build or a developer rulebook.

>
> Double earnings do not automatically mean a profitable life. A player must first spend cash on a loadout, then survive long enough or perform enough rewarded actions to earn a return. The WARDOGS Hot Zone is likely to receive pressure from all three teams because its scoring and economy benefits are public. A costly kit lost immediately may still leave the account worse off.
>
> The official wording does not reveal which reward events receive the multiplier, how rounding works, whether rewards earned just outside the boundary qualify, or whether the interface shows a separate running total. The persistent cash guide tracks the account-level system without pretending those values are final.

> Why the Hot Zone concentrates three-team conflict
>
> A normal objective edge can become quiet when two factions are committed elsewhere. The multiplier creates a visible reason for players to converge on one smaller area. Because WARDOGS has three factions, that convergence is not a predictable attacker-versus-defender fight. [01]
>
> One faction may hold the valuable ground. A second may attack it directly. The third can choose to join the same fight, cut reinforcement routes, or place enough players elsewhere in the main zone to win the broader comparison. The best choice depends on the next scoring check, the weighted count, the cost of the current lives, and how quickly reinforcements can arrive.
>
> This is an inference from confirmed rules rather than a tested competitive meta. Spawn locations, terrain, cover, building limits, vehicle prices, and communication tools will decide how the system plays in a live build.

> Can the squad reach it before the interval? A late push can spend lives without changing the point.
>
> Is there a reinforcement route? Double weight helps only while players remain in position.
>
> Does the expected cash justify the loadout risk? The correct answer cannot be reduced to “double is always better.”
