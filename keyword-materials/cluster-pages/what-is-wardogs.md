**about_the_game 正文:**

> WARDOGS is an All-Out Warfare FPS that combines tactical gunplay and combined arms combat with building and destruction mechanics in a large-scale, up to 100-player militaristic sandbox.
>
> The game mode - Inspired by ‘King of the Hill’ - has three teams fight for control of randomized 2x2km ‘Control Zones’ within larger maps. The team with the most players within the Control Zone earns points - the first team to reach 100 points wins the match. The variables in zone location and individual player tactics mean no match ever plays out the same.
>
> In WARDOGS – Every. Decision. Matters.
>
> Every player starts their journey with $10,000. Each life, you purchase a custom loadout from a wide selection of weapons, gear, utility, and vehicles to help your team win the match.
>
> Every teamplay action you perform rewards cash. From reviving squadmates, transporting friendlies to the zone, or controlling the objective. We don’t just encourage teamplay; we reward it. It’s the player’s choice how they want to play, earn and spend their profits.
>
> Cash persists from match to match, so spending big on better gear, equipment, or a vehicle at the right time has the potential to turn the tide of battle and could yield massive returns.
>
> You Choose How You Want to Play & Profit.
>
> There’s no one way to play, earn and progress. Instead, WARDOGS gives players the freedom to take on the Control Zone in any number of ways. Whether you’re a lone wolf helping out your team or part of a dedicated squad, how you approach the battlefield is entirely up to you.
>
> You could chase the 'Hot Zone' to earn double the cash, purchase a ghillie suit and snipe from a distance, or ferry supplies to teammates by helicopter. Additionally, you could choose to fortify a strategic chokepoint by building your own forward operating base, or if you're feeling destructive... man an artillery tank and rain down destruction on the enemy.
>
> There are no set rules of engagement. It’s ultimately your choice how you want to approach the battlefield.
>
> Bring the Chaos to Tactical Warfare.
> Destroy apartment blocks with rocket launchers or tear your way through towns in heavily armoured tanks. The world around you reacts to your presence. Conversely, strategize with your teammates to build upon and fortify key locations within the map for a tactical advantage.
>
> Every Moment in WARDOGS is 100% Player Driven and tied together with local voice chat to either talk tactics with teammates or troll enemy players. This is not another ‘Battle Royale’. This is not another ‘Extraction Shooter’. WARDOGS is a new Tactical take on the All-Out Warfare FPS genre that rewards decision making, communication and teamwork – and allows you to play how you want within a modern militaristic setting.

> One objective, three teams
>
> The main WARDOGS mode takes inspiration from King of the Hill. A 2 × 2 km Control Zone is placed inside a much larger 256 km² map. The team with the most players inside that zone earns points, and the first team to 100 points wins. [01]
>
> The third team changes the geometry of every fight. Two sides can be locked in a direct engagement while another force enters from a different route, attacks transport lines, or pushes the objective itself. Winning a firefight does not automatically secure the zone. Your team still needs enough players in the scoring area when the next points are awarded.
>
> The objective location is randomized. The full map is therefore a pool of possible approaches, staging areas, roads, buildings, and terrain rather than one fixed competitive lane. See Control Zone for the scoring rules currently made public.
>
> The Hot Zone concentrates the fight
>
> A smaller Hot Zone can shift within the Control Zone. Players inside it count double toward control and earn double cash. It is a multiplier, not a separate win condition. [01]
>
> That makes the Hot Zone valuable without making the rest of the battlefield irrelevant. A team still needs transport, overwatch, resupply, defensive positions, and routes into the wider objective. The smaller area simply puts a higher price on presence. The dedicated Hot Zone guide separates the confirmed rules from tactics that remain untested publicly.
>
> Every life has a price
>
> Each player starts their WARDOGS career with $10,000. Before a life, the player purchases a loadout from available weapons, equipment, utilities, and vehicles. Cash carries across matches. [01]
>
> The official examples make the intended tradeoff clear. A ghillie suit can support long-range play. A helicopter can move supplies or players. A fortified position can protect a chokepoint, while an artillery tank can attack it. These options draw from the same wider economy, so an expensive life exposes more of the player's stored balance.
>
> Cash does not come only from kills. Reviving squadmates, transporting friendly players, spotting, and controlling the objective are among the team actions described as profitable. The economy gives logistics and support work a direct account-level reward. Persistent Cash covers the confirmed structure, while How to Earn Cash lists the published reward examples.
>
> Roles come from equipment
>
> WARDOGS does not force every player into a fixed class. Players build a role through purchases and specialist items. The developers have also described six XP progression tracks, from Medic to Pilot, that support different ways to contribute. [01]
>
> The distinction is useful: a progression track can reward experience in a role without turning that role into a locked character class. Exact skill trees, unlock thresholds, item prices, and loadout limits have not been published in a complete reference. The WARDOGS XP progression guide tracks all six announced routes without inventing the four names that remain unpublished.
>
> Vehicles, building, and destruction share one battlefield
>
> WARDOGS combines infantry combat with ground vehicles and aircraft. Official material shows helicopters, tanks, transport vehicles, and infantry operating together. It also describes real-world ballistics, audio, and flight models as design targets. [01]
>
> Players can construct positions such as a forward operating base and fortify useful ground. They can also remove cover, damage apartment blocks with launchers, or push through towns in armored vehicles. Construction and destruction are tied to the objective: the randomized zone decides which bridge, building, road, or ridgeline is valuable in that match.
>
> Public material does not yet provide a complete vehicle roster, armor table, weapon list, building catalogue, or destruction model. The Combined Arms Vehicles and Building and Destruction pages record the systems without inventing missing statistics.
>
> The setting and factions
>
> The conflict takes place in Kolchia, an Eastern European setting shaped by a war over PV-1, a rare resource linked to a wider Eurasian conflict. Three factions contest the battlefield: [01]
>
> Lonestar is described as a Western paramilitary heavy hitter.
>
> Valkyra seeks to restore the Soviet People's Republic to greatness.
>
> Manticore is the Kingdom of Persia's shadow army from Tehran.
>
> These are the official high-level descriptions. Named leaders, complete histories, uniforms, arsenals, and faction-specific bonuses have not been confirmed in a public reference.
>
> Communication is part of the sandbox
>
> WARDOGS includes proximity voice alongside squad and team communication. Local voice can carry tactics between nearby teammates and interaction between opposing players. The developers have also mentioned constructible loudspeakers that broadcast faction propaganda. [01]
>
> Squads have no published size cap in the official Q&A. Squad members receive additional interface and communication tools, plus vehicle locking. See WARDOGS proximity chat for the local layer and WARDOGS squads for group and vehicle-access boundaries.
>
> What is WARDOGS not trying to be?
>
> The developer and publisher repeatedly distinguish WARDOGS from a battle royale and an extraction shooter. A match is won by reaching 100 objective points, not by being the final survivor or extracting carried loot. Persistent cash creates consequences across matches, but the public rules describe an objective shooter rather than an extraction loop. [01]
>
> The developers also say there will be no battle pass and no pay-to-win purchases. Cosmetics are planned to be earned through play, including a future Gold Market connected to Gold Bars. Monetization details can still change before release, but those statements are the current official position.
>
> What is WARDOGS offering before release?
>
> WARDOGS remains in pre-release development. The August 7–9 Closed Alpha is complete, the August 21–23 Closed Beta is next, and Steam Early Access launches September 10. Preordering guarantees Beta access; Steam Playtest offers a limited random allocation. [05]
>
> The WARDOGS reveal trailer records the original 5 February 2026 announcement, while the WARDOGS Press Kit indexes the complete 37-file official archive.
>
> The studio says the Early Access foundation will include 100-player three-team combat, persistent cash and XP, gunplay and vehicles, plus building and destruction. Account-level systems such as the Black Market, Vault, player skills, and challenges are planned to develop through Early Access.
>
> Players can follow the Early Access Status, check the WARDOGS system requirements, or apply for testing through the official community signup when recruitment is open.
