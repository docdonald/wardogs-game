## Is there a confirmed mortar in WARDOGS?

No portable mortar or finalized mortar system is confirmed in the public official material collected for this page. Team17 does mention an artillery tank as a player choice for raining destruction on an enemy position, but that is a vehicle example rather than proof of a handheld or deployable mortar. The weapon name, deployment method, and current availability are not listed in the collected sources.

This distinction prevents a common database error. A screenshot of indirect fire or an artillery vehicle cannot establish a mortar catalogue, shell type, or aiming interface. Until BULKHEAD or Team17 publishes a named mortar entry or a reproducible live build exposes one, the page should remain a status guide rather than an invented tutorial.

## What does the artillery-tank example confirm?

The official Team17 page lists manning an artillery tank and using it to pressure enemy positions as one way to play. This confirms that WARDOGS includes a remote or indirect-fire role in its broader combined-arms sandbox. It does not provide a model name, range, reload time, crew requirement, shell damage, or friendly-fire rule.

Use the example to explain how fire support relates to the Control Zone. A long-range platform may help break a fortified route while infantry, logistics, and medics work closer to the objective. The official source does not say that artillery is always available, always effective, or tied to a particular faction.

## Can the official weapon system support indirect fire?

Steam describes WARDOGS as combining tactical gunplay, vehicles, destruction, and a per-life loadout purchase. That structure leaves room for several fire-support tools, but the public store page does not enumerate them. Planned expansion also mentions additional weapon categories, which confirms that the current public catalogue is not final.

Do not fill the gap with real-world mortar tables. A game may simplify elevation, wind, ammunition, spotting, and reload for playability, even when its design references real-world ballistics. Any exact calculation, map grid, or shell cost is not listed in the collected sources.

## How does fire support connect to the Control Zone?

The Control Zone is won through sustained team presence, not by destroying the most buildings. Fire support should therefore be judged by whether it opens a route, breaks a defensive position, protects a logistics run, or forces another faction away from scoring space. A barrage that creates no objective advantage can spend cash without helping the team.

The Hot Zone can offer double cash while concentrating risk, so indirect fire may be tempting but also harder to validate. The official sources do not publish a Hot Zone artillery bonus, target restriction, or ammunition multiplier. Keep the advice strategic and label all detailed behavior not listed in the collected sources.

## Are mortar controls or ammunition published?

No. The collected official pages do not publish mortar keybinds, a fire mode, elevation controls, map ranging, ammunition types, resupply, or a cost. The Steam community can contain test observations, but those posts are not a stable official specification and may describe a different build.

The same caution applies to the word “artillery.” It may refer to a specific tank, a future category, or a broader marketing description. Until the developer names the system, do not merge artillery tank data into a mortar entry.

## What should players do now?

Players interested in indirect fire should learn the confirmed combined-arms loop: read the objective, coordinate with the squad, protect supplies, and use the vehicles and weapons that are actually available in the current build. If an artillery tank appears in the shop, verify its current interface and role before spending persistent cash. If no mortar appears, do not download a third-party “unlock” or config file.
