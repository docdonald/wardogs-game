## What do WARDOGS attachments do?

The official Steam listing confirms that WARDOGS lets players build a custom loadout from weapons, gear, utility, and vehicles. The collected weapon references describe attachments as part of a wider handling and weight model, not as a guaranteed list of fixed stat upgrades. Exact attachment names, slots, prices, and effects must therefore be checked against the current game.

The safest approach is to select an attachment for a clear task: sight picture, controllability, range, or a weight tradeoff. ## How should you compare attachments?

Start with the weapon’s intended engagement distance and the terrain around the Control Zone. An optic may help identify targets at range, while a lighter setup may feel more practical when moving with a squad. The public materials do not publish a complete handling formula, so compare changes through repeatable in-game tests rather than assuming a community chart is universal.

Weight is an important part of the collected design reference. It can affect the balance between weapon capability and the rest of your loadout, but the exact movement or stamina penalty is not confirmed. Treat the choice as a role decision: a rifle built for overwatch, a close fight, and a vehicle crew may not need the same attachments.

## How do attachments interact with persistent cash?

Every life begins with a loadout purchase, and cash persists between matches. That makes a high-cost attachment an economic decision as well as a weapon decision. If a configuration helps you win a key fight or protect a transport, its value may be different from the same configuration in a low-risk scouting life.

WARDOGS also rewards revives, transport, spotting, and objective control, so an attachment should support the way you earn cash. A player carrying a heavy overwatch build may provide information and cover, while a lighter build may reach the zone or revive a teammate faster. Exact prices and cash returns are not fully published.

## Beginner attachment checklist

Choose a clear purpose, keep the total loadout cost visible, and test the weapon at the distance where you expect to fight. Compare the setup before and after one change, then decide whether the handling gain is worth the weight or cash tradeoff. Do not copy an “ultimate build” until its source, game version, and role assumptions are clear.
