## What counts as equipment in WARDOGS?

The official Steam description groups weapons, gear, utility, and vehicles into the custom loadout purchased for each life. Equipment is therefore broader than a gun: it includes the tools and specialist choices that help a squad reach, hold, or contest the Control Zone. The full item list and exact costs are not published in the collected sources.

That makes the correct beginner question “what problem does this purchase solve?” rather than “which item is always best?” A utility item that helps a revive, transport, or defensive position can be more valuable than a higher-cost weapon in the wrong fight. Exact effects, prices, capacity limits, and unlock rules are “待确认” unless the current build documents them.

## How does equipment connect to the cash economy?

Every player starts with the published $10,000 balance, buys a loadout for each life, and keeps cash between matches. Equipment competes with weapons and vehicles for that same budget. A loadout should therefore be judged by the value it creates for the team and the risk it exposes if the life ends early.

The official examples include a ghillie suit for long-range play, a helicopter for moving players or supplies, and a forward operating base for fortifying a position. These examples show different roles without proving a fixed class system. The best purchase depends on the objective, squad plan, and current cash balance.

## Which equipment supports a squad?

Revive and transport tools directly support named team actions. The official page says reviving squadmates and transporting friendlies to the zone can reward cash, while the wider material also identifies spotting and logistics as useful contributions. Equipment that makes those actions safer or more reliable can create value even when the player is not leading the kill count.

Fortification and supply choices connect equipment to the wider battlefield. A squad can protect a chokepoint, move resources, or establish a position near a randomized objective. The exact inventory, cooldowns, and specialist restrictions have not been confirmed, so avoid presenting community role names as locked character classes.

## How should you choose equipment for a role?

Start with the squad’s immediate task. A player moving with the objective may want a manageable weapon and utility that keeps them mobile; a support player may prioritize revives, transport, or supplies; a defensive player may value equipment that helps hold a position. The sources confirm the role ideas but not a final class menu or skill-tree requirement.

The equipment choice can change between lives. Because cash persists, a player can save for a high-impact purchase rather than spending every balance on one attempt. Track what the item enabled—an objective hold, a safe revive, a transport run, or a successful retreat—before deciding whether it earned its cost.

## Are there confirmed equipment stats?

No complete official equipment table appears in the collected material. Prices, weight, armor, range, cooldowns, capacity, durability, and role restrictions are not all published. Even if a competitor page lists an item name, it should be treated as a reference lead rather than proof of the current Early Access value.

This is especially important for guides that promise a “best loadout.” WARDOGS is still evolving, and balance, progression, maps, vehicles, and utility can change. Keep a future item entry tied to a source, a build date, and the specific role or objective it serves.

## Beginner equipment checklist

Pick one clear team job, buy only the gear that supports it, and leave enough cash for a second life. Stay near the squad when carrying revive or transport value, and use voice communication to avoid duplicating the same specialist purchase. If the objective moves, reassess the loadout plan instead of defending an item choice that no longer helps.

The confirmed foundation is flexible equipment-driven roles inside a persistent cash economy. The complete catalogue and stat values are “待确认,” so this page favors decision principles over invented numbers.
