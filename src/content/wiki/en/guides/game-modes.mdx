## Which WARDOGS Game Modes are confirmed?

The public record currently supports one core WARDOGS mode: a three-team Control Zone ruleset inspired by King of the Hill. Up to 100 players contest a randomized 2×2 km objective, the team with the strongest presence earns points, and the first team to reach 100 wins. The sources do not publish a separate finished list of ranked, casual, training, or rotating modes.

This core mode is larger than a simple capture point. Every life uses a purchased loadout, cash persists between ordinary matches, and vehicles, construction, destruction, logistics, revives, and local voice all feed the battle. The game’s identity comes from how these systems interact around the objective rather than from a long menu of unconfirmed queues.

## How does WARDOGS use King of the Hill ideas?

Official material says the mode is inspired by King of the Hill, and the competitor research links that design lineage to ARMA 3’s KOTH tradition. WARDOGS uses the familiar idea of fighting for a central objective, but the published rules add three factions, a larger battlefield, randomized zone placement, persistent cash, purchased lives, combined arms, and a Hot Zone incentive.

That makes WARDOGS a standalone ruleset, not an ARMA server, port, remake, expansion, or replacement for a specific community mode. A guide can explain the lineage to returning KOTH players while still teaching the WARDOGS-specific economy and three-team pressure. Details borrowed from another game are not evidence for WARDOGS.

## What is the Control Zone match flow?

A match revolves around locating the active Control Zone, moving people and equipment into useful positions, and maintaining presence while two opposing factions contest the same area. The published scoring description says the faction with the most players in the zone earns points, with a 30-second tick and first-to-100 win condition in the current evidence. Tie handling and every boundary rule remain “待确认.”

The wider battlefield changes how teams reach the objective. Helicopters can transport players or supplies, construction can protect a route, and destruction can remove cover. A player can also earn cash through revives, transport, spotting, combat, and objective work, so a useful life does not need to be measured only by eliminations.

## Is WARDOGS a battle royale or extraction shooter?

No. The official Steam and Team17 descriptions explicitly distinguish WARDOGS from both formats. Players fight for Control Zone points, can respawn, and use a persistent cash economy that carries between ordinary matches; the win condition is the faction reaching 100 points before the other two teams.

That answer does not mean the game has no risk. A purchased loadout is tied to each life, and expensive spending can reduce future options. The risk is an economic and tactical layer around the objective, not an extraction requirement to leave the map with loot or a battle-royale elimination circle.

## How do Hot Zone and persistent cash fit the mode?

The Hot Zone is described as an inner area that can award double cash and, in current competitor evidence, carry greater objective weight. It increases the reward and exposure around the main objective, but it is not a separate mode. Persistent Cash starts at the officially stated $10,000, pays for future loadouts and vehicles, and rewards teamplay actions.

Cash and Control Zone score remain separate resources. A revive or transport can earn cash and help put more teammates into the zone, while a kill may pay without directly creating objective presence. The official sources do not publish complete prices, action payouts, or a guaranteed best mode-specific build.

## What role do vehicles, building, and destruction play?

WARDOGS presents combined arms as part of the core sandbox. Players can use vehicles for transport, logistics, or combat; build fortifications and forward positions; and destroy structures or cover to change a route. These systems are not optional decorative features in the public description, but their final costs, capacities, damage values, and balance are not fully published.

The correct tactical choice depends on the current zone and the team’s problem. Buying a vehicle without a route or crew can waste persistent cash, while a modest support action can create the presence needed to score. Treat all precise vehicle or construction recommendations as version-dependent until official data becomes available.

## Will more modes arrive during Early Access?

The roadmap and Steam FAQ promise more gameplay variety and objective variations during Early Access. They do not name a complete future mode list or guarantee a dated schedule. New modes, private-server options, training tools, or ranked queues should be added only when an official announcement identifies them.

For now, a reliable Game Modes page should lead with the Control Zone answer, explain the King of the Hill inspiration, separate WARDOGS from battle royale/extraction, and keep future modes under a clearly marked “待确认” section. That structure serves both new players and KOTH veterans without overstating the roadmap.
