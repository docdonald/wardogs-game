## What is WARDOGS in one sentence?

WARDOGS is a tactical all-out warfare first-person shooter for up to 100 players. Three teams fight over a randomized Control Zone while buying weapons, equipment, utilities, and vehicles with cash that persists between matches. The official description combines tactical gunplay, combined arms, construction, destruction, logistics, and local voice chat in a large-scale sandbox.

Steam lists BULKHEAD as the developer and Team17 as the publisher. The current collected release information places the game in Steam Early Access on September 10, 2026. The base and Supporter Edition prices, console plans, and future features should always be checked against the live store because the game is still evolving.

## How does the three-team objective work?

The main mode is inspired by King of the Hill. A randomized Control Zone is placed inside a much larger map, and the team with the most players inside earns points. The first team to reach 100 points wins, so eliminating opponents is useful only when it helps your team control the scoring area.

The third faction changes the shape of every fight. Two teams may be contesting the zone while the other attacks a route, protects a transport line, or arrives from another direction. The exact map roster and future objective variations are not completely published, so only the current Control Zone structure should be treated as confirmed.

## What is the Hot Zone?

The collected official description identifies a smaller Hot Zone that can concentrate the fight. Players inside it count double toward control and earn double cash in the sourced reference material. It is a reward multiplier inside the wider objective, not a separate win condition.

That distinction matters for new players. A squad may need transport, overwatch, resupply, or a defensive route around the wider Control Zone even when the Hot Zone is valuable. Exact timing, scoring UI, and all Hot Zone rules should be rechecked against the live game because public material is pre-release.

## How does cash shape each life?

The official pages say each player starts with $10,000 and buys a custom loadout for every life. Cash persists from match to match, so a weapon, vehicle, utility item, or support plan can affect later decisions. The economy is designed to make every purchase meaningful without requiring a player to use the same role every round.

Cash comes from team actions as well as combat. Reviving squadmates, transporting friendly players, spotting, and controlling the objective are named examples, while logistics and supply movement are part of the same support loop. The exact payout table, item prices, and unlock rules are not published in a complete official reference.

## Are there fixed classes or roles?

WARDOGS does not present a single locked character-class system in the collected sources. Players form a role through the equipment and specialist items they purchase, and the developer has described six XP progression tracks that support different contributions. Medic and Pilot are confirmed track names in the source material; the full list of names and thresholds is not complete.

This means “role” can describe how you are playing rather than a permanent character selection. A player can support a squad with revives, move people or supplies by helicopter, build a forward position, or focus on direct combat. Keep role tracks, persistent cash, and match score separate because they represent different systems.

## How do vehicles, building, and destruction connect?

WARDOGS puts infantry, ground vehicles, aircraft, and logistics on the same battlefield. Official examples include helicopters moving people or supplies, armored vehicles pushing through towns, and artillery attacking fortified positions. The randomized objective determines which bridge, road, building, or ridgeline matters in a particular match.

Players can build and fortify useful positions, while rockets and heavy vehicles can damage or remove cover. The sources confirm the direction of the system, but not a complete vehicle roster, armor table, building catalogue, or destruction model. ## What is WARDOGS not?

The developer and publisher repeatedly distinguish WARDOGS from a Battle Royale and an Extraction Shooter. Matches are decided by objective points rather than final-survivor status or extraction loot. Persistent cash creates consequences between lives and matches, but the public rules describe an objective-focused team shooter.

The game is also not presented as a single correct playstyle. A lone player can help a squad, a dedicated group can coordinate a push, and support or logistics can earn cash. Local voice chat connects nearby players and teams, but the complete voice range, squad limits, and loudspeaker rules remain version-dependent.

## What is confirmed before release?

The confirmed high-level package is a 100-player, three-team tactical FPS with Control Zone scoring, persistent cash, loadouts, vehicles, building, destruction, and local voice. Steam Early Access is the documented release route. A Closed Beta and future Playtest signup are separate pre-release access paths, not substitutes for the Early Access date.

Details such as the final faction bonuses, complete weapon and vehicle lists, exact XP unlocks, full map catalogue, console availability, and 1.0 date are not all confirmed.
