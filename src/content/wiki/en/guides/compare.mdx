## What can you compare in WARDOGS?

The defensible WARDOGS Compare scope is weapons, equipment, and vehicles. Steam confirms that each life uses a purchased custom loadout containing weapons, gear, utility, and vehicles, while Team17 describes tactical gunplay, combined arms, transport, construction, and destruction. A useful comparison therefore starts with role, target, evidence, and purchase context instead of inventing a damage table.

The current public sources do not publish a complete item catalogue, prices, damage, range, weight, capacity, armor, or vehicle performance sheet. Those fields should remain “待确认” until a named Early Access build or official data source supports them. The goal is to help players choose for a battlefield problem without presenting guesses as facts.

## How should a comparison page be structured?

The competitor comparison tool uses three steps: choose a category, select two to four assets, and read the published differences. It currently separates Weapons, Equipment, and Vehicles, then shows matching and different fields with an evidence label for each one. That structure keeps the user’s question focused and allows a page to say when two assets share a property or when the source has not published it.

For a static article, begin with the player task: general-purpose infantry, demolition, medical support, transport, or another role explicitly supported by the sources. Then list confirmed observations and separate editorial interpretations. A phrase such as “medium range” should be labelled as a reading of footage, not a numeric falloff value.

## What can be said about weapons?

Official descriptions and footage identify assault-rifle-style infantry use and rocket launchers as tools that can destroy structures. Those are role-level distinctions, not a full balance comparison. The current competitor comparison records general-purpose infantry versus demolition focus, with projectile and ammunition systems noted in developer material but without public damage values.

Do not call one weapon “best” for every player. A general-purpose rifle may answer close or medium infantry pressure, while a launcher may solve a structure or cover problem, but the final range, cost, recoil, damage, reload, and ammunition limits are not published. The correct recommendation is conditional on the Control Zone, cover, squad plan, and available cash.

## What can be said about equipment?

The official game description names gear and utility as part of each-life loadouts. The collected comparison examples include bandages, a medkit, and a ghillie suit, while the broader sources also mention building items and larger backpacks. These examples show different support and concealment purposes, but they do not establish a complete equipment taxonomy or a fixed class system.

Compare equipment by the problem it solves: recovery, concealment, construction, carrying capacity, or another documented use. Prices, healing amounts, duration, camouflage statistics, inventory slots, and unlock requirements remain unpublished. If an item appears only in promotional footage or a test build, record the build and evidence type rather than treating it as a final catalogue entry.

## What can be said about vehicles?

Vehicles are part of WARDOGS combined arms and can support transport, logistics, or direct combat. Team17 and Steam describe helicopters, armored vehicles, artillery-style support, and vehicle purchases as examples of how players can approach the battlefield. The exact vehicle roster, crew size, speed, armor, weapon systems, fuel, price, and repair behavior are not all publicly documented.

A vehicle comparison should therefore begin with role and evidence: transport, logistics, armored pressure, artillery, or air support when that role is directly sourced. A visible seat or weapon in a trailer is not proof of final capacity or performance. The current comparison model correctly marks unpublished values instead of filling them with assumptions.

## How does Persistent Cash affect comparisons?

Every player starts with the officially stated $10,000, buys a loadout for each life, and keeps cash between ordinary matches. That makes price and replacement risk important comparison fields, but the public sources do not expose a complete vendor list or action payout table. A “better” item that costs more may be a poor choice when the squad needs repeated lives or a transport route.

Hot Zone double cash, revives, transport, spotting, combat, and objective work can all affect the balance, but none supplies a universal item ranking. Compare an asset’s documented role and evidence first, then add price or efficiency only when a versioned source publishes it. This keeps the comparison connected to the actual economy without manufacturing precision.

## What evidence labels should be used?

The competitor tool separates Confirmed statements, Official visual observations, Archive labels, restrained Wiki readings, and Not published values. This is a useful vocabulary for WARDOGS Compare. “Confirmed” means a publisher, store, or developer states it; “Official visual” means it is visible in official footage; “Wiki reading” means a cautious editorial classification; “Not published” means the developer has not supplied the value.

Keep one evidence label per field rather than one confidence label for an entire asset. A weapon’s role can be confirmed while its final model name and damage remain unpublished. That detail lets readers compare what is actually known and prevents a high-confidence role description from silently implying a complete stat sheet.

## What should remain unconfirmed?

The open fields include final model names, prices, damage, falloff, recoil, projectile behavior, armor, speed, weight, capacity, repairs, ammunition, ownership, and version-specific availability. The official roadmap may add assets and alter balance during Early Access, so every future value needs a build date.

The direct answer is that WARDOGS Compare can already organize role-level differences across weapons, equipment, and vehicles, but it cannot honestly provide a complete numeric tier list. Build comparisons from the collected sources, embed official footage for context, and leave missing fields clearly marked until the game publishes them.
