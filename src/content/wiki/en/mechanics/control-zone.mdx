## What is the WARDOGS Control Zone?

The WARDOGS Control Zone is the match objective: three teams fight for player presence inside a randomized 2×2 km area, and the first team to reach 100 points wins. This is not a simple elimination race. The team that places more players in the active zone earns the scoring advantage, while cash, transport, revives, construction, and vehicle support help that team stay there.

The zone sits inside a much larger battlefield. Team17 describes a 256 km² map and a changing objective location, so the route to the zone is part of the problem. A squad may need to cross open ground, use a vehicle, build a position, or destroy cover before it can hold the objective. Exact boundary behavior and every possible zone location are not publicly documented yet.

## How does Control Zone scoring work?

Control Zone scoring is based on presence rather than a fixed number of kills. When one faction has the strongest presence, that faction receives the objective point described in the current official material. The public rule set also describes a 30-second scoring tick and a first-to-100 win condition, but the exact handling of a tie or a contested tick should be rechecked in the Early Access build.

This distinction matters for decision-making. A player who leaves the area to chase an isolated opponent may gain a combat opportunity while reducing friendly presence. A transport pilot, builder, spotter, or medic can be contributing to the same win condition by keeping more teammates alive and getting them back into the zone. The scoreboard and the personal cash balance measure different things.

## Why are there three teams?

WARDOGS places Lonestar, Valkyra, and Manticore in the same match rather than using a two-sided frontline. That creates more than one threat direction around the objective. A faction that pushes one opponent can expose its own flank to the third team, and a short-term alignment of interests does not create a formal alliance.

The three-team structure also makes the zone a moving information problem. Players need to read which faction has the strongest presence, which route is being reinforced, and whether a fight outside the zone is helping or delaying the objective. No public source confirms fixed faction bonuses or different scoring rules for the three named factions, so the teams should be treated as competing sides of the same published ruleset.

## How does the larger map change the objective?

The 2×2 km Control Zone is the focused objective inside the wider 256 km² battlefield. The larger space gives vehicles, logistics, terrain, and construction a practical role: a team must move people and supplies into the fight, establish useful positions, and respond when the active location changes. The published dimensions describe the relationship between the battlefield and the objective; they do not prove that every square kilometre is equally accessible in every match.

Map variation is part of the intended replay value. Team17 says the zone is randomized, while the Early Access plan describes three maps with three variations each. The sources do not yet define whether variation changes terrain, objective locations, weather, or building layouts. Treat map names observed in a test build as test evidence until the launch roster is confirmed.

## What is the Hot Zone’s role?

The Hot Zone is a smaller, higher-risk area within the Control Zone rather than a separate match mode. Official material says players inside it can earn double cash, and the current competitor record also describes increased objective weight. Because the reward attracts all three factions, the Hot Zone can concentrate pressure even when a team already controls the wider zone.

The double-cash rule should not be confused with double victory points unless the current build confirms that behavior. The published sources clearly connect the Hot Zone to cash and describe its objective multiplier in the current evidence, but they do not provide a stable radius, duration, or stacking formula. Use the Hot Zone page for the separate risk-and-reward details.

## Which actions help a team hold the zone?

Control Zone play rewards more than shooting. Reviving squadmates, transporting friendlies to the objective, spotting threats, moving supplies, and controlling the area are all named or supported team actions in the collected sources. These actions can increase the number of friendly players who reach the zone and can also return cash to the individual player.

Vehicles and construction are tools for solving a specific objective problem. A helicopter can move players or supplies, a fortified chokepoint can protect an approach, and destruction can remove cover or open a route. The public sources do not publish exact prices, vehicle capacities, build costs, or damage values, so a universal “best” Control Zone loadout is not confirmed.

## What should a new player do first?

Start by locating the active zone and joining the nearest useful squad plan. Buy a loadout you understand, keep enough cash for another life, and choose a task that supports presence: move with the infantry, revive, transport, spot, build, or protect a route. The objective is won by sustained team presence, not by proving that every life must produce the most eliminations.

Do not treat one test clip or one successful route as a permanent rule. Zone location, balance, scoring details, and map variations may change during Early Access. Until the game publishes a complete rules reference, rely on the first-to-100 structure and the three-team objective that the official sources consistently support, and label all finer timing or boundary details as version-dependent.
