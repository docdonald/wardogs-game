## What is the WARDOGS Black Market?

The Black Market is a planned out-of-match metagame within WARDOGS. Official descriptions frame it as a way for an account to create value through arms dealers, wagers, businesses, production, and supply crates. It is not confirmed as a completed launch-day shop or a current in-match vendor.

The feature is intended to sit beside Player Skills, Challenges, Gold Bars, and the Vault. The public sources do not publish a final interface, inventory, prices, eligibility, or trading rules. Those fields remain unpublished.

## What activities are planned?

The developer’s examples include an arms dealer who can smuggle weapons or ammunition, betting on a difficult objective in a future match, and businesses that produce items while the player is offline. Another planned path lets players buy a weapon or vehicle manufacturer and produce custom loadouts or supply crates at a lower cost than an in-match vendor.

These are design examples, not instructions for a live money-making method. The sources do not provide success probabilities, operating costs, production times, item lists, or guaranteed margins. The sources do not list every number or availability condition.

## How does Black Market connect to the Vault?

The official video describes Gold Bar items, weapons, or vehicles obtained through the Black Market entering a personal Vault. Those items are planned to be redeemable for free in a future match, and Vault capacity is described as having a permanent upgrade path. This links the market to long-term account value rather than only the current life.

The final Vault size, upgrade costs, item loss rules, redemption limits, and season carryover are not listed in the collected sources. A concept description does not prove that every item can be stored or redeemed in the same way. Treat the exact flow as unavailable.

## Is Black Market the same as Gold Market?

No. The Gold Market is associated with Gold Bars and cosmetics, while the Black Market is described as a broader planned metagame involving production, wagers, dealers, and Vault items. They may share account systems, but the collected sources do not define them as one store.

Do not combine their inventories or assume that a cosmetic purchase works like a weapon or vehicle redemption. Official announcements and the final interface should determine the relationship. Real-money purchases, player-to-player trades, and regional rules remain unpublished.

## How should players read the planned economy?

Separate immediate match spending from account-level plans. Cash buys the weapons, gear, utility, and vehicles used in a life, while the Black Market is intended to create longer-term value between matches. A business, wager, or manufacturer may eventually support a strategy, but the evidence does not justify a guaranteed return.

When the feature becomes playable, record the item, cost, production time, risk, and redemption result by version. Do not turn a developer example into a static profit guide before the game publishes those fields. The current safe answer is a feature overview with a clear scope note.

## What remains unconfirmed?

Inventory, prices, production costs, wagers, success rules, offline timing, player trading, real-money access, Vault capacity, upgrades, item loss, season resets, redemption limits, and launch timing are not listed in the collected sources. The Black Market may change during Early Access as the metagame is built.

The direct answer is that WARDOGS Black Market is a planned optional metagame connecting dealers, production, wagers, businesses, supply crates, and the Vault. Its direction is public; its live economy and rules are not.
