## How does WARDOGS Persistent Cash work?

WARDOGS Persistent Cash is an individual account balance that carries from match to match. Official Steam and Team17 material says every player starts with $10,000, buys a custom loadout for each life, and earns cash through combat and teamplay. The balance supports the Control Zone fight, but it is not the faction score and it does not replace the first-to-100 objective.

The system makes each purchase a longer-term decision. A weapon, support item, or vehicle can solve the current battlefield problem, yet the same spending reduces what is available for the next life. The public sources confirm the loop but do not publish a complete price list, exact payout table, account floor, or death-recovery system.

## What does the $10,000 starting balance mean?

The $10,000 starting balance is the clearest published number in the economy. It gives every player a common starting point before the first custom loadout. It does not tell us how many rifles, medical kits, ammunition loads, or vehicles that balance can buy because the official sources do not expose a complete vendor catalogue.

That limitation matters for guide writing. We can describe a player as having a starting cash balance, but we cannot call a particular build “cheap,” “expensive,” or “optimal” without a named version and verified prices. Any recommended budget tiers, break-even calculations, or universal first-loadout tables are not listed in the collected sources.

## How are loadouts purchased?

Each life begins with a purchase and build decision. The documented categories include weapons, gear, utility, and vehicles, and WARDOGS does not use a fixed class lock that forces every player into one role. Equipment and behavior define what a player contributes during that life: medical support, transport, construction, reconnaissance, infantry pressure, or another task supported by the available kit.

The purchase system links personal economy to team needs. A squad that lacks transport may value a vehicle more than another rifle; a team that needs revives may value medical equipment; a push that must cross a defended route may need mobility or destruction. The sources do not publish exact inventory limits, refund rules, repair costs, or what happens to unused supplies after death.

## Which actions earn cash?

Official material names kills, revives, spotting, transporting friendlies, and objective work as cash-earning routes. Team17’s examples emphasize that the economy rewards useful teamplay rather than only eliminations. This creates more than one path to rebuilding a balance after spending: a player can support the faction by moving people, recovering teammates, providing information, or holding the objective.

The amount earned for each action is not publicly confirmed. A guide can safely list the action categories and explain why they matter, but it should not publish a payout ranking or promise that a particular role always earns more. The current evidence also does not specify whether rewards scale with distance, danger, duration, streaks, or match result.

## Does the Hot Zone pay more?

Yes, the official game description says players can chase the Hot Zone to earn double cash. That rule is an economy incentive, not a guarantee of profit. The Hot Zone also concentrates the three factions and can increase the chance that an expensive life is lost before its reward is collected.

Cash and Control Zone points remain separate. Standing inside the objective can help a faction score, while the same player may receive personal cash for objective work. A transport or revive can earn cash and increase future objective presence without directly adding a scoreboard point at that instant.

## What happens between matches and seasons?

The ordinary match rule is persistence: cash remains available for later matches. The collected roadmap and economy sources also describe a planned seasonal boundary where Cash and XP reset and remaining Cash automatically converts to Gold Bars. Gold Bars and certain cosmetics are described as permanent across seasons, but the exchange rate, reset date, limits, and final interface are not listed in the collected sources.

Pre-Alpha test cash is a separate case. The official FAQ says cash earned in early testing will not carry into the full game. That temporary test rule should not be confused with the seasonal economy planned for Early Access, and a page should label both statements by their evidence period.

## How should players manage risk?

Treat cash as operational reach rather than a score to maximize at all costs. When the zone route and enemy pressure are unclear, use a loadout you can explain and preserve flexibility. When the squad has a defined need—transport, medical recovery, construction, reconnaissance, or anti-vehicle pressure—spend for that need instead of buying unrelated equipment.

High-cost purchases can change a critical fight, but the public evidence does not reveal the correct threshold. Do not infer insurance, resale, refunds, or recovered equipment from promotional footage. Record the build, purchase, reward events, and after-life balance in a public version before turning a community observation into a numerical strategy.

## What is confirmed and what is missing?

Confirmed facts are the $10,000 starting balance, per-life custom purchases, named combat and support reward routes, Hot Zone double cash, ordinary match persistence, and the planned seasonal Cash/XP reset with Gold Bar conversion. Missing fields include complete prices, exact payouts, limits, loss behavior, season dates, and the transfer rules from Early Access to version 1.0.

That evidence boundary is part of the answer. Persistent Cash gives WARDOGS a meaningful economy around a three-team objective, but it is still being developed.
