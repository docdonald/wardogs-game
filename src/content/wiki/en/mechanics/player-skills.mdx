## What are WARDOGS Player Skills?

Player Skills are planned as a persistent account progression layer. The developer describes players ranking up, earning skill points, and investing those points according to how they want to play. This is separate from the immediate Cash purchase that builds a loadout for the next life.

The feature is part of the Early Access metagame plan, not a published final skill tree. Official material does not provide the complete branches, point costs, level cap, or launch version. Those details remain 待确认.

## How do XP tracks relate to Player Skills?

The official FAQ describes six XP progression tracks and uses Medic and Pilot as examples of role directions. Player Skills are described in the later metagame plan as a way to spend skill points across play styles. The public sources do not prove that each XP track maps one-to-one to a final skill branch.

This distinction matters because XP, Cash, and Control Zone score measure different things. Cash funds a loadout, XP supports longer-term progression, and the team score decides the match. A future build may connect them in a specific way, but the formula is 待确认.

## Which skill examples are public?

The developer video gives example directions: a Medic may revive faster, Infantry may improve weapon handling, and a Pilot may gain better fuel efficiency. These examples show the intended relationship between play style and skill investment. They are not published percentages, final names, or guaranteed class choices.

The official FAQ also says roles are player-defined rather than permanently locked. A player can choose equipment and actions that support a role in a given life, while Player Skills are intended as longer-term account choices. Exact skill effects, stacking, and balance are 待确认.

## Are Medic, Infantry, and Pilot fixed classes?

No. The collected sources use Medic, Infantry, and Pilot as role examples, while the FAQ emphasizes player-defined roles and purchased equipment. A player is not confirmed to spawn with a permanently locked class or a mandatory skill branch. The terms should be treated as play-style labels until the final UI says otherwise.

This lets a squad adapt to the active Control Zone. One life may prioritize revives, another transport, another direct combat or support. Whether a skill investment limits future loadouts, can be reset, or affects all roles is 待确认.

## Are Player Skills permanent across seasons?

The official planning language describes Player Skills as permanent account value, in contrast to seasonal Cash and XP resets. That suggests an account-level progression layer intended to persist longer than one season. It does not publish the exact carryover rule into version 1.0 or future seasons.

Do not promise that every point is permanent or refundable. Reset, respec, account transfer, balance changes, and skill removal rules are not documented in the collected sources. Keep those claims marked 待确认.

## How should players prepare for the system?

Play the role that helps the objective and observe which actions the current build actually rewards. A medic-style support life, pilot transport run, infantry push, logistics route, or another confirmed contribution can inform future progression choices. There is no verified fastest-XP method or universal best skill path yet.

Keep Cash spending separate from skill planning. A skill point may be long-term account value, while a vehicle or specialist item still requires the current life’s loadout budget. Update the guide when official UI, patch notes, or reproducible build data provide the missing numbers.

## What remains unconfirmed?

The six track names, full skill tree, point costs, XP formula, level cap, effect values, role mapping, respec rules, balance impact, seasonal carryover, and launch date are 待确认. Medic, Infantry, and Pilot are examples, not a final class list. No official source publishes a complete Player Skills table.

The direct answer is that WARDOGS Player Skills are planned persistent progression: rank up, earn skill points, and invest by play style. The system’s direction is public, but the final tree and numbers are not.
